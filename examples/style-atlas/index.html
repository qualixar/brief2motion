Rules, motion, accessibility and when to choose it

Signature rules

  • Borders 2 to 4 px, solid, usually black
  • Shadow is one solid colour, offset about 4 px, blur 0
  • Two or three saturated fills on a pale ground
  • Headlines about twice the body size, neutral sans for body text
  • Padding 24 to 32 px so dense shapes can breathe

Motion that fits

Fast and physical: a button moves by its shadow offset on hover and lands on press (100 to 200 ms). Marquee strips and stickers tilted a degree or two suit it. No soft fades.

Choose it for

Creator tools, indie products, developer tools, events, consumer brands that want a voice.

Avoid it for

Banking, healthcare, dense enterprise screens.

Accessibility

  • Loud colour pairs still have to pass contrast: yellow with cyan does not
  • Condensed or decorative faces hurt paragraphs; keep body text plain
  • Every control needs its border, or it stops looking pressable

Rules, motion, accessibility and when to choose it

Signature rules

  • Fill about 15 to 40% opaque; a dark tint keeps white text readable when a bright shape drifts behind it
  • backdrop-filter: blur of about 10 to 25 px (NN/g shows 25 px keeping edges readable; 100 px turns the background to mush)
  • A 1 px light border and a soft drop shadow to lift the panel
  • Needs something colourful behind it, or there is nothing to frost

Motion that fits

Slow drift of the shapes behind the glass, and a small lift on hover. Keep the blur radius fixed: animating blur is expensive.

Choose it for

Navigation bars, overlays and cards over pictures or gradients.

Avoid it for

Long reading text, data tables, low-end devices.

Accessibility

  • Text over a see-through panel has uneven contrast: test against the lightest and darkest thing that can sit behind it
  • Give a solid fallback for prefers-reduced-transparency (experimental) and prefers-contrast: more
  • An ancestor with opacity, filter, mask or clip-path limits what the blur can see

Rules, motion, accessibility and when to choose it

Signature rules

  • Glass only on the floating layer: bars, buttons, sheets
  • Clearer than frosted glass: light blur, strong saturation, a bright top rim and an inner glow
  • Pill and capsule shapes
  • Over bright content, add a dark dimming layer (Apple suggests 35%)

Motion that fits

Springy scale on press, and controls that merge or split. Keep it to the control layer.

Choose it for

Media and photo products, Apple-platform companions, floating toolbars.

Avoid it for

Text-heavy pages, anything where the glass would sit on the content itself.

Accessibility

  • Legibility was the main complaint about the real thing: check text on glass against busy content
  • Honour reduced transparency and increased contrast with a solid fill
  • Do not call a web imitation "Apple Liquid Glass"

Rules, motion, accessibility and when to choose it

Signature rules

  • Element and background share one colour
  • Two shadows: light towards the top left, dark towards the bottom right, about 8 px offset and 16 px blur
  • Pressed state swaps to inset shadows
  • Pastel, low-contrast palette

Motion that fits

A press swaps the raised shadow for an inset one in about 150 to 250 ms.

Choose it for

A few decorative controls: a music player, a smart-home dial.

Avoid it for

Forms, data-heavy apps, anything that must be scanned quickly.

Accessibility

  • Its defining trait is low contrast: control edges fail WCAG 1.4.11 (3:1) unless you add a border or a strong icon
  • Raised and pressed states look alike to many people
  • Keep text itself at 4.5:1

Rules, motion, accessibility and when to choose it

Signature rules

  • Corner radius about 30 px or more
  • Two inner shadows (light on top, darker below) plus one large outer shadow tinted with the shape colour
  • Strong, cheerful accent colours on a pale ground

Motion that fits

Squash and stretch on a spring: a button flattens slightly on hover and bounces on press.

Choose it for

Children and education products, playful consumer apps, onboarding screens.

Avoid it for

Finance, legal, anything that should feel sober.

Accessibility

  • Needs strong accents and real contrast to avoid the neumorphism trap
  • In dark mode the fill cannot be black or the shadows vanish

Rules, motion, accessibility and when to choose it

Signature rules

  • Gradients that imitate a light from above
  • Bevels: a light top edge and a dark bottom edge
  • Material textures and real-world metaphors
  • Pressed states that actually move down

Motion that fits

Mechanical: a key travels 2 to 3 px and returns, a switch slides, a dial turns. Short and with no bounce unless the object has one.

Choose it for

Audio tools and instruments, games, products where a physical metaphor teaches the use.

Avoid it for

Content sites, large design systems that must scale.

Accessibility

  • The metaphor gives strong affordance, which flat design lost
  • Texture behind text lowers contrast: check it
  • Ornament must not hide state

Signature rules

  • Solid colour, no gradients, no shadows
  • Simple geometric icons and plenty of space
  • Hierarchy from size, weight and colour only

Motion that fits

Colour and opacity changes of 100 to 200 ms. Movement is rare and short.

Marketing sites, dashboards, documentation, anything that must load fast.

Products that rely on a physical metaphor.

  • With no depth cues, clickable things can look like labels: keep underlines, borders or fills on every control
  • A Nielsen Norman Group study is often cited for flat interfaces being slower to use for that reason

Rules, motion, accessibility and when to choose it

Signature rules

  • Colour roles (primary, on-primary, surface, on-surface) generated from a seed colour
  • Corner tokens: 4, 12, 16, 28 px and full
  • Motion tokens: durations from 50 to 1000 ms in steps, standard easing cubic-bezier(.2, 0, 0, 1)
  • State layers: a tint of the content colour at 8% on hover and 12% on press

Motion that fits

Use the scale: 100 to 200 ms for small changes, 300 to 500 ms for larger ones, with the standard easing. Expressive adds springs.

Choose it for

Android and Google-ecosystem products, utility apps, teams that want a ready system.

Avoid it for

Brands that must not look like an Android app.

Accessibility

  • Colour roles are designed in contrast pairs: use on-colours with their own background
  • Google's own research found Expressive designs were found faster, but dropping familiar patterns hurt usability

Signature rules

  • A modular or 12-column grid, visibly obeyed
  • One sans-serif family in two or three sizes

Signature rules

  • Convert the image to greyscale first
  • Map shadows to one colour and highlights to another (multiply for the dark, lighten or screen for the light, or an SVG feComponentTransfer)

Avoid it for

Accessibility

  • Text over a duotone photo needs a scrim or a solid panel

Rules, motion, accessibility and when to choose it

Signature rules

  • Background near black, not pure black; text off-white, not pure white
  • Depth from lighter surfaces, not from shadows
  • Accent colours less saturated than on white
  • Ship a light theme too, switched by prefers-color-scheme

Motion that fits

The same as the light theme. Glows and gradients read stronger on dark, so use less.

Choose it for

Developer tools, media and video, games, dashboards used in dim rooms.

Avoid it for

Long-form reading for a general audience, unless a light theme is one click away.

Accessibility

  • Light mode performs better for most people in most conditions: offer both and respect the system setting
  • Pure white text on pure black causes halation for some readers
  • Borders need 3:1 against the surface to be seen

  • nngroup.com

Visual treatments

Isometric

Objects drawn as if seen from above at an angle with no perspective, so parallel lines never meet. Reads as a tidy little model world.

Rules, motion, accessibility and when to choose it

Signature rules

  • Three visible faces per object: top lightest, one side mid, one side darkest
  • True isometric uses 30° to the horizontal; pixel-friendly art uses a 2:1 slope
  • One light direction for every object
  • Flat colours; depth comes from the three tones

Motion that fits

Parts lift, stack and slide along the three axes. Layers separating vertically explain how a product is built.

Choose it for

Explaining systems, infrastructure, logistics, fintech flows, feature diagrams.

Avoid it for

Emotional or lifestyle brands.

Accessibility

  • Isometric scenes are illustrations: describe what they show in text
  • Small isometric labels get hard to read at an angle; keep text flat

Sources

  • en.wikipedia.org

Motion-led approaches

These four are ways of using motion, not looks, so each has a whole example site in the brief2motion repository.

  • Kinetic typography

    The words are the animation: letters, words and lines move, stretch and take turns, so reading and watching become one thing.

    Example site: relay

  • Scroll-driven storytelling

    The page is a sequence: sections pin, pictures scrub and charts redraw as the reader scrolls, so the scroll bar becomes the timeline.

    Example site: tidewater

  • 3D / immersive WebGL

    A real-time 3D scene is the page: a product you can turn, a world the camera moves through, particles that form a shape.

    Example site: halden-one

  • Micro-interactions

    Small animated answers to what a person just did: a toggle that slides, a row that makes room, a number that settles.

    Example site: mise
