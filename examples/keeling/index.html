Mauna Loa Observatory, Hawaii · annual means, 1959 to 2025

One instrument, sixty-seven years, a line that has not once gone down.

Since 1958 an analyser on the side of a Hawaiian volcano has measured how much carbon dioxide is in the air. Scroll, and the chart beside the text draws what it found.

1959: the first full year

Charles David Keeling's analyser had been running since March 1958. Its first complete calendar year averaged 315.98 parts per million: of every million molecules of dry air, about 316 were carbon dioxide.

1988: past 350

The yearly average went up every single year. In 1988 it reached 351.69, the first annual mean above 350 parts per million.

2015: past 400

Twenty-seven years later the annual mean was 401.01. The first fifty took 29 years. The second fifty took 27.

2025: 427.35

The latest full year. That is 111.37 parts per million more than 1959, a rise of 35.2% over the length of the record.

And it is speeding up

Same numbers, cut another way: the average rise per year in each decade. In the 1960s the air gained under one part per million a year. Since 2019 it has gained more than two and a half.

The data

Every number on this page comes from one public file: NOAA's annual mean CO₂ at Mauna Loa, compiled by Dr. Xin Lan (NOAA/GML) and Dr. Ralph Keeling (Scripps Institution of Oceanography). The decade averages are computed in the page from the same numbers. From December 2022 to July 2023 the readings come from nearby Maunakea, because an eruption had closed the Mauna Loa site.
