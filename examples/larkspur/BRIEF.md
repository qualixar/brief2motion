# Brief: Larkspur

A launch site for Larkspur, a shared inbox for teams of two to ten people. Larkspur is a made-up product, invented to show brief2motion's site mode: every name, price and feature on the site is fictional and the site says so, in the open, near the top and in the footer.

Who it is for: the person on a small team who is tired of "who answered that?". Mail sent to the team address lands in one place, and every message belongs to one person until it is answered.

What visitors must be able to do: understand it in a few seconds, compare the two plans, and start the free trial with a work email.

Pages: the home page, a pricing page, and a "start your trial" page with a form.

Facts the site may state, and nothing beyond them:
- Larkspur is a shared inbox for teams of two to ten people. Mail to the team address lands in one place.
- Each message has one owner. A teammate "claims" it, and the owner shows on the row.
- Internal notes stay inside the team and are never sent to the sender. Saved replies can be inserted into a reply.
- Two plans, billed monthly, cancel at any time, prices in US dollars per person per month. Team: US$12, up to 5 inboxes, 30 days of history. Studio: US$24, unlimited inboxes, full history.
- A 14-day free trial, no card.
No customers, no logos, no reviews, no ratings, no statistics about speed or satisfaction. There are none.

Look: calm and paper-like. Warm paper, ink navy, one violet for actions. A serif for headlines, the system sans for text. No stock photos: the product is shown by a small drawing of the inbox itself, in code, marked as sample data.

Motion: one moment on the home page. A message arrives in the inbox and a teammate claims it (about a second and a half, once). Buttons give a small press response. Nothing else moves. With reduced motion the finished state is shown.

Done when: a visitor on a phone can go from the home page to a finished trial form, including after one mistake; the plans can be compared in a table that reads on a 320 px screen; nothing about the product is shown as real; every site-mode check passes.
